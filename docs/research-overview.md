# AGIS - Research Overview



**AI Agent Governance & Inventory System**



## 1. Research Objective



AGIS explores the governance challenges organizations face as they adopt increasingly capable AI agents.



The research focuses on how enterprises can improve visibility, accountability, risk review, and lifecycle governance for AI agents.



## 2. Key Problem Areas



### Agent Inventory and Visibility

Organizations need ways to identify and maintain records of AI agents operating across their environments.



### Ownership and Accountability

Governance processes should establish who is responsible for an agent, its intended purpose, and its lifecycle.



### Permissions and Connected Resources

AI agents may interact with APIs, databases, internal applications, and external services. Understanding these connections is important for governance.



### Risk Assessment and Review

Organizations need structured processes to identify risks and review agents before granting permission for production use.



### Lifecycle and Auditability

Agent creation, review, modification, approval, and retirement should be traceable.



## 3. Governance Frameworks Under Study



AGIS research considers established governance and security frameworks as reference points:



- **NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF):** A reference for understanding and managing AI-related risks.

- **ISO/IEC 42001:** A reference for AI management systems.

- **ISO/IEC 27001:** A reference for information security management.

- **EU AI Act:** A regulatory reference relevant to organizations developing or deploying AI systems within its scope.



Referencing these frameworks does not mean AGIS is certified, compliant, or independently assessed against them.



## 4. Existing Technology Landscape



The broader ecosystem includes several categories of tools:



- AI gateways and observability platforms.

- Cloud security and access management systems.

- IT asset and configuration management platforms.

- AI risk management and compliance solutions.

- Developer infrastructure and agent frameworks.



AGIS is exploring how a registration-first governance layer could complement these categories through agent identity, ownership, lifecycle records, and review workflows.



This is a product research direction, not a claim that existing products lack these capabilities.



## 5. AGIS Research Direction



The project is investigating:



1. Developer-native agent registration through `agis.yaml`.

2. Better visibility into agent ownership and connected resources.

3. Governance workflows across an agent's lifecycle.

4. Approaches to identifying unmanaged or Shadow AI agents.

5. Connections between agent inventory and runtime governance.

6. Practical enterprise adoption and integration requirements.



## 6. Research Limitations



This document represents the project's research direction and working hypotheses.



It is not a completed market study, formal regulatory analysis, independent security assessment, or proof of product-market fit.



Further technical research, customer interviews, and validation are required.
